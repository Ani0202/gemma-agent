**BLOCKING**

1. **`memguard` signals processes outside the allowlist.** [bin/memguard:190](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/bin/memguard:190) includes every descendant, without rechecking its marker, RSS, cwd, or session exemption. A marked parent can therefore cause an unmarked, small child with cwd `/` to be killed. A mocked snapshot reproduced this: target set `{500}`, signalled PID `501`. Apply the allowlist to every signal recipient, including descendants and the final root.

2. **PID reuse can redirect signals and OOM adjustments to unrelated processes.** [bin/memguard:168](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/bin/memguard:168) and [bin/memguard:194](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/bin/memguard:194) operate on bare PIDs after classification. No process start time or pidfd binds subsequent operations to the inspected process. The root check at [bin/memguard:206](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/bin/memguard:206) verifies only UID, allowing a replacement, same-UID session process to be killed. Use identity-bound signalling and identity-checked `/proc` access; revalidate eligibility before acting.

3. **Orphans and concurrent forks can survive while the watchdog reports `survivors=[]`.** [bin/memguard:142](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/bin/memguard:142) groups processes by current ancestry, so an already orphaned marked process becomes a separate job. During killing, [bin/memguard:190](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/bin/memguard:190) tracks descendants of the original root plus the original `known` list; newly forked children can be reparented before either scan sees them. There is also no rescan after the three-second root grace period. Finally, [bin/memguard:214](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/bin/memguard:214) checks only PIDs successfully signalled, rather than all job members. A mocked fork/reparent sequence left PID `502` alive with reported survivors `[]`. Persistent job identity, repeated membership discovery, and an independent final survivor inventory are needed.

4. **The wrapper patch trusts stale process-group numbers.** [ops/patches/bin-job-hardening.diff:59](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/ops/patches/bin-job-hardening.diff:59) reads a stored PGID and signals it without validating the original wrapper’s identity or current group membership. A stale `running` status after a crash/reboot can target an unrelated group when that number is reused. Validate process identity and group ownership before signalling; the three-field fallback needs equivalent PID protection.

Killing wrapper → child leaves a grandchild reparented elsewhere, invisible to the subsequent `pgrep -P`. Likewise, a new job’s descendant that calls `setsid()` escapes the recorded group. [ops/patches/bin-job-hardening.diff:72](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/ops/patches/bin-job-hardening.diff:72) misses both cases and can report success. Scope-stop errors are also ignored. Preserve descendant identities before terminating parents, check escaped members, and verify scope termination when applicable.
