2. **Signals remain vulnerable to PID reuse after discovery.** At [patch:88](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/ops/patches/bin-job-fallback.diff:88) and [patch:91](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/ops/patches/bin-job-fallback.diff:91), `$(marked)` completes the entire `/proc` scan before signalling its first PID. Bind recipients to process identities, preferably using pidfds as `codex/memguard-fix:bin/memguard:211` does, and avoid escalating against an unconditionally cached PGID.

3. **Legacy records still permit the stale-identity kill this patch addresses.** At [patch:83](/home/ubuntu/work/gemma/ops/patches/bin-job-fallback.diff:83), an unrelated process that reuses the old wrapper’s PID and leads that same numbered group passes the four-field check. These are inherited gaps, but remain unresolved under the requested legacy review. Without trustworthy identity evidence, refuse destructive legacy signalling rather than treating numeric PID/group membership as proof.
